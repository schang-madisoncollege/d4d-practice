## The exercise

Work through these in order. Commit as you go rather than at the end. The
history is the deliverable.

1. Put this folder under version control and make your first commit.
2. Push it to GitHub. Open it in a browser to confirm it arrived.
3. Look at your commit on github.com. If the name or the picture beside it is
   not yours, your Git email does not match your GitHub account. Fix it before
   you go further.
4. Fix one of the two TODO comments in `index.html`. Either one. Commit it.
5. Make a branch. On that branch, change something visible about the header in
   `styles.css`. Commit it, switch back, then merge it in.
6. Break something on purpose in a file you have not committed yet, then get
   the committed version back.
7. Commit a change you do not want, push it, then undo it with a new commit
   rather than by deleting anything.
8. Write your recovery note at the bottom of this file, then push everything.

Five commits is the floor. More is better and they cost nothing.

---

## Commit messages

One line, present tense, says what changed. Under about 60 characters. One
change per commit so the line stays honest.

Works:

- `add tool card markup to the tools page`
- `fix nav link pointing at the wrong page`

Does not:

- `update`
- `final v2 FINAL fixed`

---

## Recovery note

About 150 words. Answer these:

- What did you break, and was it on purpose?
- Which command brought it back?
- What did you expect to happen that did not?

Write it in your own words. A note that says "I broke it and then I fixed it"
tells me you got through the exercise. A note that says which command you
reached for first and why it was the wrong one tells me you understand what
happened.
